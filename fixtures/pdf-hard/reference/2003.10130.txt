                                                                                     Abstract
                                                  Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are gaining increasing attention on graph data
                                                  learning tasks in recent years. However, in many applications, graph may be
                                                  coming in an incomplete form where attributes of graph nodes are partially un-
                                                  known/missing. Existing GNNs are generally designed on complete graphs which
                                                  can not deal with attribute-incomplete graph data directly. To address this prob-
                                                  lem, we develop a novel partial aggregation based GNNs, named Partial Graph
                                                  Neural Networks (PaGNNs), for attribute-incomplete graph representation and
                                                  learning. Our work is motivated by the observation that the neighborhood aggrega-
                                                  tion function in standard GNNs can be equivalently viewed as the neighborhood
                                                  reconstruction formulation. Based on it, we define two novel partial aggregation
                                                  (reconstruction) functions on incomplete graph and derive PaGNNs for incomplete
                                                  graph data learning. Extensive experiments on several datasets demonstrate the
                                                  effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed PaGNNs.

in which Mij = 0 indicates that Hij is missing/unknown and Mij = 1 otherwise, as illustrated in
Figure 1. Existing GNNs are generally developed on attribute-complete graph data G(A, H) which

Related Works. To deal with attribute-incomplete graph G(A, H, M), one popular way is to first
employ some imputation/completion methods to estimate and fill in the missing values and then
adopt traditional GNNs to conduct graph learning. For example, Yoon et al. [10] propose Generative
Adversarial Imputation Nets (GAIN) to complete missing data by adapting a GAN [11] framework.
Chen et al. [9] recently develop Structure-Attribute Transformer (SAT) for attribute-missing graph
completion and then utilize GNNs [1, 4] for incomplete graph node classification. Obviously, this
kind of methods conduct missing value estimating and GNN learning independently, which usually
leads to weak sub-optimal learning. To overcome this limitation, Taguchi et al. [8] recently propose
an end-to-end model GCNMF which uses a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to estimate the missing
data and jointly learns the GMM and GNN’s parameters together. However, GCNMF [8] increases
the amount of training parameters and thus has high computational complexity.
Contributions. Different from previous works, this paper presents a straightforward partial
message-propagation scheme on incomplete graph to develop novel Partial Graph Neural Networks
(PaGNNs) for incomplete graph data learning. Specifically, the proposed partial message propagation
is motivated by the observation that the neighborhood aggregation operation in traditional GNNs
can be equivalently viewed as the neighborhood reconstruction optimization. Based on it, we derive
a partial reconstruction formulation and develop a novel partial message-propagation scheme for
GNN’s layer-wise propagation. Overall, the main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows,

      • We re-interpret neighborhood aggregation in GNNs from neighborhood reconstruction
        perspective and derive a novel partial aggregation function for GNN’s layer-wise message
        propagation on incomplete graph.
      • We develop a simple and effective Partial Graph Neural Networks (PaGNNs) to deal with
        incomplete graph data representation and learning.

Experiments on several datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed PaGNNs.

Cora and Citeseer datasets and 64 on AmaPhoto and AmaComp datasets. All network parameters are
optimized by using Adam algorithm[17] in which the training is stopped if the validation loss does
not decrease for 100 epochs, as utilized in many previous works [8, 1].

4.2   Comparison Methods

We first compare our PaGNNs with some commonly used imputation based methods which include
Mean Filling (MF) [20], VAE [21] and GAIN [10]. These methods first estimate and fill in the
missing values for the incomplete graph and then use GCN [1] on the ‘filled’ complete graph.
We also compare PaGNNs against some recent competitive methods including Structure-Attribute
Transformer (SAT)[9] and GCNMF [8]. The detail of these comparison methods are introduced below.

       • MF [20] fills in all the missing values in attributes H by using the mean value of the known
         elements across different nodes.
       • VAE [21] utilizes a VAE model to recover the missing values in attributes H.
       • GAIN [10] uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) [11] technique to generate the
         missing values in attributes H.
       • SAT (GCN) [9] first estimates the missing attributes via a GNN-based distribution matching
         model and then uses standard GNNs [1, 4] for incomplete graph data learning.
       • GCNMF [8] employs a learnable Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to fill in the missing data
         and learns all parameters in an end-to-end manner.

5                                   Conclusion

In this paper, we develop a novel PaGNNs for attribute-incomplete graph data learning and repre-
sentation. PaGNNs adopt GCN-like message propagation scheme and thus can be implemented as
simply and efficiently as standard GNNs. PaGNNs conduct on incomplete graph directly and do
not need some additional parameters to estimate missing values. Extensive experiments show the
superiority of our PaGNNs against other comparison methods.
